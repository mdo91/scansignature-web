You can sign a PDF on iPhone without printing it. Open the PDF, add a signature, position it on the right page, and save a signed copy. Apple's Preview app supports signing PDF forms. If you want to capture the signature you normally write on paper, Signature Scan: Sign PDFs (SignDocs) can photograph that handwriting, remove the paper background, and place it on your PDF.

The SignDocs workflow below requires iOS 26 or later, or iPadOS 26 or later on iPad. It adds an image of your handwritten signature. It does not provide identity verification or a certificate-based digital signature.

## Before you start

Save the PDF somewhere you can open it in Files. If it is an email attachment, download it first. Check which pages need a signature and whether the recipient also expects information you must fill in using another tool. Signing a PDF does not automatically complete its form fields.

You will also need either a signature already saved in SignDocs or a piece of plain paper and a pen to capture one.

## Sign with your handwriting in SignDocs

1. Open SignDocs and choose the option to open an existing PDF from Files.
2. Select your document. Check its title and page count so you do not sign the wrong version.
3. Choose a saved signature, or capture one from paper. For a new signature, use the camera guide, crop closely, and compare the Clean, Balanced, and Bold results.
4. Choose the PDF page that contains the signature line.
5. Drag the signature onto the line. Pinch to resize it so it fits without covering the surrounding text.
6. Review the page at a readable size, then use **Save File** to create a new signed PDF.
7. Open the saved copy and check the signature before sending it.

Need help with the capture step? Follow the [handwritten signature scanning guide](/blog/scan-handwritten-signature-iphone/). The aim is to preserve your actual ink strokes rather than draw a different version with your finger.

## Use Apple's built-in option

In the Preview app on iPhone, open your PDF form and use the form-filling controls. Apple's guide explains how to add a signature or text form box with the Add button. This is useful when the built-in signing method already meets your needs.

The tools and menu positions depend on your OS version. Use Apple's current instructions rather than assuming a screenshot from an older iPhone tutorial will match your screen. SignDocs is an alternative for the specific workflow of capturing handwriting from paper and reusing it on PDFs.

## Check the final PDF

Review the exported file, not just the editing screen. Look at the signature's size, page placement, and readability. Make sure that no required page is missing and that the PDF opens again after saving.

SignDocs saves a new signed copy while preserving the original PDF. Give the output a clear name so that an unsigned original is not attached by mistake. The [save and share guide](/blog/save-share-signed-pdf-iphone/) covers that last step.

## What if the recipient requires a digital signature?

An embedded handwritten signature and a certificate-based digital signature are different things. If a portal or recipient specifies a certificate, identity check, or a particular signing service, use their required process. Do not assume that placing a signature image satisfies it.

For everyday paperwork where the recipient accepts a handwritten signature image, scanning the entire printed document is unnecessary: work from the original PDF and save a separate signed copy.
