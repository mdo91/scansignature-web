# Guidance of improvement

## Purpose

Improve the chances that useful search visits lead to downloads of **Signature Scan: Sign PDFs**, also known as **SignDocs**, by Damas Technologies LLC. Treat organic growth as an experiment: technical correctness, keyword estimates, and publishing activity do not establish that rankings or downloads will improve.

Use this guidance alongside the [growth playbook](growth-playbook.md), [Keyword Planner integration](keyword-planner.md), and [organic-growth skill](../.agents/skills/organic-growth/SKILL.md). Their publication limits, factual requirements, validation steps, and permission boundaries continue to apply. Keep operational evidence and results in the private, ignored `.growth/` directory.

## 1. Improve existing guides with evidence of product value

Start with the page that already serves the reader's task. Add a new guide only when the intent is distinct and the existing library cannot serve it well. The approved daily target is one qualified new guide per language, at most eleven changed guides total; quality skips and eligible substantial-update fallbacks are reported separately.

For each proposed improvement:

- State the reader's problem and give a direct answer before introducing the app.
- Explain when Apple's built-in tools are sufficient and when a verified Signature Scan capability is useful. Apple Preview already supports filling and signing PDFs, so a general signing tutorial needs additional value beyond repeating those instructions.
- Where possible, document an actually performed workflow with genuine screenshots or a short demonstration. Suitable subjects include capturing a handwritten signature, removing its paper background, comparing extraction modes, and preserving supported ink colors or fine strokes.
- Use before/after examples only when the original input and actual output are available. Identify relevant conditions and limitations; do not imply that one example proves universal quality.
- Keep the iOS/iPadOS 26 requirement visible near relevant instructions and download prompts. Explain applicable export limits and connection requirements accurately.
- Add a contextual download link where the demonstrated capability helps the task, using the existing locale-specific campaign token.

Ready to publish means the page adds a useful answer or demonstration beyond what already exists, all product steps are verified, and the editorial attribution describes the evidence honestly. Supplied screenshots alone do not establish hands-on testing. If stronger evidence is unavailable, retain that limitation or keep the proposed material in draft.

## 2. Select topics by installation intent and realistic opportunity

Assess United States/English, Türkiye/Turkish, Germany/German, and Spain/Spanish separately. Use only the matching market's Search Console data and fresh Keyword Planner cache. Missing or suppressed metrics are unavailable, not zero; one market's missing data must not block another's useful work.

Before choosing a topic, record privately:

- **Intent:** What task does the person need to complete, and why might it require an app installation?
- **Demand evidence:** Which relevant queries, existing page impressions, customer questions, or researched backlog items support the topic? Label keyword volumes as estimates and preserve ranges.
- **Current search results:** Inspect relevant results in the intended language and market. Identify their format, completeness, built-in alternatives, and missing practical help. Record the observation date and targeting limitations. This inspection is not a dependable exact-rank report.
- **Product advantage:** Which verified capability makes the proposed answer useful? If there is no clear reason to install Signature Scan, treat the page as a support resource rather than a leading acquisition opportunity.
- **Existing coverage:** Review related guides across all locales. Improve the existing intent owner instead of creating pages for close keyword variants.
- **Local usefulness:** Review native phrasing, translated instructions, interface labels, examples, and calls to action. Translation parity alone does not establish local demand or editorial quality.

Prefer a well-supported problem with a clear product connection and a demonstrably better answer over a broad query with a larger estimated volume. Advertising competition is not organic ranking difficulty. Search Console average position is an aggregate metric, not an exact keyword rank.

Preserve existing slugs and canonical URLs. Use explicit translation groups and reciprocal hreflang only for genuine published equivalents; independent language libraries are allowed. Do not multiply near-identical pages merely to increase coverage.

## 3. Add deliberate, useful distribution

For each substantial improvement, identify an audience and an appropriate way for that audience to discover it. Options include a short product demonstration on an existing owned channel, a relevant community contribution, or a resource that an editor would have a genuine reason to reference.

- Share a concrete answer or demonstration, with a relevant guide as supporting material.
- Favor evidence that is useful to others, such as a clear capture example or a documented troubleshooting workflow.
- Keep promotion accurate and proportionate. Do not buy ranking links, fabricate endorsements, or post repetitive promotional replies.
- Record distribution suggestions privately. Contacting editors, community members, or other people requires explicit authorization; this document does not authorize sending messages or creating accounts.
- When distribution is authorized, record the channel and date so later changes can be interpreted with context. Preserve existing attribution links and use only authentic tokens.

The goal is qualified discovery and legitimate references. Publishing another article is not a substitute for helping the right audience find an existing useful one.

## 4. Measure search visits alongside app outcomes

Use the existing measurement tools before proposing additional analytics. Follow the playbook's complete recent 28-day and prior 28-day comparison windows, reporting buffer, and separate market filters.

Review these stages without assuming that one proves the next:

1. **Search discovery:** Indexing status and relevant query/page impressions.
2. **Website visits:** Search Console clicks and CTR, with page and device context. Track relevant non-brand searches separately where available.
3. **App Store activity:** Product-page views and first-time downloads attributed to the matching campaign, when Apple makes them available.
4. **Product value:** Retained use or paid outcomes only where reliable existing reporting supports them.

Campaign links do not provide a complete record of website button clicks. Apple applies reporting thresholds and attribution rules; withheld campaign data does not mean zero downloads. Do not calculate a conversion rate from mismatched Search Console and Apple campaign populations or date windows. Do not infer iOS compatibility from a mobile device category alone.

Keep market results distinct. Report what changed, the source and dates, what remains unavailable, and plausible alternative explanations. Search changes, seasonality, distribution, and app changes can coincide; an observed increase does not establish that a content edit caused it. Do not add website analytics, paid services, or Google permissions without the required authorization.

## 90-day review plan

Start the review period when an improvement is published and its deployment is verified. Record the baseline and publication date privately. A reporting window that ends before publication cannot measure its effect.

- **Around day 30:** Check discovery and indexing, relevant query intent, mobile usability, and the download path. Fix confirmed blockers before expanding the library.
- **Around day 60:** Compare complete, equivalent windows within each market. Review whether titles match the task and whether visitors have a clear, demonstrated reason to install. Improve the strongest existing opportunities.
- **Around day 90:** Decide which topics and markets deserve continued effort using qualified search clicks and available app outcomes. Continue promising work, rework weak intent or unclear differentiation, and defer expansion without a useful hypothesis. If evidence remains sparse, report that uncertainty and define the next review question.

These are review checkpoints, not deadlines for ranking gains. Google says changes may take hours to several months to appear, and some changes produce no noticeable effect. Do not promise a download target without an observed baseline and defensible assumptions.

## Evidence behind the guidance

- [Google: creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) supports original value, demonstrated experience, and usefulness beyond rewriting other sources.
- [Google: SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) explains discovery, promotion, uncertain outcomes, and the time needed to assess changes.
- [Apple: fill out and sign PDF forms on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/fill-out-and-sign-pdf-forms-iphd7e3c0c74/26/ios/26) establishes the built-in alternative that signing content must account for.
- [Apple: campaign links](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect-analytics/acquisition/campaign-links) explains App Store attribution and reporting thresholds.
- [Google: Saramin case study, published in 2020](https://developers.google.com/search/case-studies/saramin-case-study) illustrates sustained technical improvements measured alongside signups. Its established-platform results are not a forecast for this app.
- [Buffer: evergreen content and promotion, published in 2018](https://buffer.com/resources/increase-website-traffic/) describes ongoing updates and distribution developed over years. It is a historical company account, not proof of a repeatable growth rate.
